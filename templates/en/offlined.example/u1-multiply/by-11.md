To **multiply a two-digit number by 11**, split its digits and put **their sum** in the middle. So 32 × 11 = 352, because 3 + 2 = 5.

## Why it works

Multiplying by 11 is multiplying by 10 and adding the number once more: 32 × 11 = 320 + 32. When you add them, the tens digit of 32 lands right under the units digit of 320, which is why the sum of the two digits appears in the middle.

::: ejemplo Example: 53 × 11
::: pasos
Split the digits: 5 … 3
In the middle, their sum: 5 + 3 = 8
Result: **583**
:::
:::

## When the sum is more than 9

If the two digits add up to 10 or more, **carry** the tens digit of the sum to the digit on the left. For example, in 78 × 11 the sum is 7 + 8 = 15: write the 5 in the middle and add 1 to the 7, so the result is **858**.

::: intenta
a) Work out 26 × 11.
b) Work out 69 × 11.
???
a) 2 + 6 = 8: **286**.
b) 6 + 9 = 15: write the 5 and carry 1 to the 6: **759**.
:::

::: resumen
- To multiply a two-digit number by 11, put the sum of its digits in the middle.
- If the sum is 10 or more, carry 1 to the digit on the left.
:::
